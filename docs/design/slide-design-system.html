Rationale

Where this comes from

A style guide should not be invented; it should be excavated from work that already has a voice.

This system is rooted in johnguerra.co and the brief: modern, elegant, academic, HCI / InfoVis, clean, professional, with sparse, presenter-support slides on a light-but-not-white ground, its own identity (Northeastern as a credit).

So the DNA carries over directly: Playfair Display for headings and Lato for body (the site's exact pairing), a navy × orange signature (the course bar + badge), blue for links, and a categorical InfoVis palette echoing the bubble charts — reserved for diagrams. Red is retired; orange is the single warm spark.

01
Principles

Six ideas, everywhere

Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, Proximity — plus hierarchy and restraint. The grammar every slide obeys.

C

Contrast

One thing is biggest, darkest, or orange — never two. Navy on paper for calm; orange only where the eye must land.

R

Repetition

Same eyebrow, same rule, same margins on every slide. Consistency is what makes a deck read as one voice.

A

Alignment

A single left edge for text; nothing floats. Invisible lines connect the eyebrow, title, and body.

P

Proximity

Group what belongs together, separate what doesn't. White space is structure, not leftover.

H

Hierarchy

Serif title → large statement → quiet caption. The size order is the reading order.

·

Restraint

One idea per slide, few words, generous space. If a mark doesn't carry meaning, cut it.

04
Space & grid

Air is the material

An 8px rhythm, a generous 64px margin, and a reserved base. Sparse slides need the empty space to do the work.

8px

Base unit

All spacing is a multiple of 8 (8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64). One rhythm, no arbitrary gaps.

64px

Slide margin

A 64px frame on every edge; content never touches the bezel. The eyebrow marks the top-left origin.

≡

One left edge

Eyebrow, title and body share a single left alignment. Centered layout is reserved for title, divider, and quote.
